# Knowledge level definition 

- **Low-level** = factual information such as recall, which can be remembered but cannot be used in new situations
- **High-level** = problem-solving/transfer knowledge that is applied, used, and can be organized into an integrated representation

# Bloom's domains of learning

**Cognitive domain:** Learning related to thinking, knowledge, understanding, and mental processes.
**Affective domain:** Learning related to emotions, attitudes, values, and beliefs.
**Psychomotor domain:** Learning related to physical movement, hands-on abilities, and practical skills.

# ABCD method definition

Every objective must contain all four ABCD components, identifiable as
separate fields as well as woven into one natural-language `statement`:

- **Audience** — who performs the behavior (e.g. "the learner", "a new
  support agent").
- **Behavior** — a single, observable, measurable action described with ONE
  Bloom-aligned verb. Never use allowed verbs and never use banned verbs.
- **Condition** — the circumstances or resources under which the behavior is
  performed (e.g. "given a live production incident", "using the internal
  style guide", "without reference material").
- **Degree** — the standard used to judge success (e.g. an accuracy
  threshold, a time limit, a required number of correct instances,
  adherence to a named rubric).

## Pro tips

- Audience can be omitted when always "the learner" — implied.
- Degree can be omitted when always 100% — implied.
- **Behavior + Condition are non-negotiable.**
- **One action verb per objective.** One objective = one sentence.

## Banned verbs

- ❌ understand
- ❌ remember
- ❌ know
- ❌ appreciate
- ❌ learn
- ❌ be familiar with
- ❌ be aware of
- ❌ be conscious of
- ❌ master

## Allowed verbs (examples)

- ✅ identify, define, list, describe, classify, distinguish, characterize, predict, estimate (low-level / recall)
- ✅ apply, calculate, demonstrate, solve, implement, construct, conduct, produce, formulate (mid)
- ✅ evaluate, assess, design, critique, compose, review, approve, audit, inspect, measure (high-level / problem-solving)

# Validity and Reliability

Every learning objective must be valide and reliable:

- **Validity** — it accurately measures and addresses the intended learning objectives
- **Reliability** — it produce consistents results when used with similar learners under similar conditions
